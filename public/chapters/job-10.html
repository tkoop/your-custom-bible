Job’s Plea to God

1“I loathe my own life;

I will express my complaint

and speak in the bitterness of my soul.

2I will say to God:

Do not condemn me!

Let me know why YouyouYou prosecute me.

3Does it please YouyouYou to oppress me,

to reject the work of YouryourYour hands

and favourfavourfavor the schemes of the wicked?

4Do YouyouYou have eyes of flesh?

Do YouyouYou see as man sees?

5Are YouryourYour days like those of a mortal,

or YouryourYour years like those of a man,

6that YouyouYou should seek my iniquity

and search out my sin—

7though YouyouYou know that I am not guilty,

and there is no deliverance from YouryourYour hand?

8Your hands shaped me and altogether formed me.

Would YouyouYou now turn and destroy me?

9Please remember that YouyouYou molded me like clay.

Would YouyouYou now return me to dust?

10Did YouyouYou not pour me out like milk,

and curdle me like cheese?

11You clothed me with skin and flesh,

and knit me together with bones and sinews.

12You have granted me life and loving devotion,a

and YouryourYour care has preserved my spirit.

13Yet YouyouYou concealed these things in YouryourYour heart,

and I know that this was in YouryourYour mind:

14If I sinned, YouyouYou would take note,

and would not acquit me of my iniquity.

15If I am guilty, woe to me!

And even if I am righteous, I cannot lift my head.

I am full of shame

and aware of my affliction.

16Should I hold my head high,

You would hunt me like a lion,

and again display YouryourYour power against me.

17You produce new witnesses against me

and multiply YouryourYour anger toward me.

Hardships assault me

in wave after wave.

18Why then did YouyouYou bring me from the womb?

Oh, that I had died, and no eye had seen me!

19If only I had never come to be,

but had been carried from the womb to the grave.

20Are my days not few?

Withdraw from me, that I may have a little comfort,

21before I go—never to return—

to a land of darkness and gloom,

22to a land of utter darkness,

of deep shadow and disorder,

where even the light is like darkness.”

12 a Forms of the Hebrew chesed are translated here and in most cases throughout the Scriptures as loving devotion; the range of meaning includes love, goodness, kindness, faithfulness, and mercy, as well as loyalty to a covenant.
